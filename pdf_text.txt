the

Introduction

and

Strategic

Context:

The

Military

"Datafication

of

the

World"

The

contemporary

battlefield

and

global

geopolitical

environment

are

undergoing

a

structural

mutation

of

unprecedented

scale,

characterized

by

massive

digitization,

increasing

interconnection

of

weapon

systems,

and

the

return

of

high-intensity

conflict.

The

exponential

multiplication

of

sensors—whether

observation

satellites,

drones,

space

and

maritime

radars,

electromagnetic

interception

systems,

or

open-source

intelligence

(OSINT)

streams—generates

a

gigantic

daily

volume

of

heterogeneous

data.

This

military

hyper-connectivity

is

part

of

what

defense

strategists,

such

as

Thomas

Gomart,

call

the

"datafication

of

the

world".

In

this

new

paradigm,

operational

superiority

no

longer

relies

exclusively

on

kinetic

firepower

or

armored

maneuvers,

but

on

an

armed

force's

ability

to

capture,

process,

analyze,

and

exploit

information

faster

and

more

accurately

than

its

adversary.

However,

this

unprecedented

profusion

of

data

simultaneously

creates

a

new

critical

vulnerability:

cognitive

overload.

Colossal

flows

of

raw,

often

unstructured

information

structurally

exceed

human

analytical

capacities.

Collecting

petabytes

of

tactical

and

strategic

data

is

useless

if

intelligence

analysts

or

staff

officers

lack

the

necessary

tools

to

sort,

correlate,

and

extract

relevant

intelligence

within

timeframes

compatible

with

real-time

decision-making.

Former

Armed

Forces

Minister

Florence

Parly

summarized

this

situation

by

stating

that

massive

data

processing

constituted

"the

new

sinews

of

war".

It

is

to

answer

this

existential

challenge

of

data

massification

that

the

Direction

générale

de

l'armement

(DGA)

of

the

French

Ministry

of

Armed

Forces

initiated,

in

November

2017,

the

ARTEMIS

project

(Architecture

de

Traitement

et

d'Exploitation

Massive

de

l'Information

multi-Sources

et

d'Intelligence

Artificielle).

Far

from

being

just

a

simple

software

tool

or

a

database,

ARTEMIS.IA

is

conceived

as

a

true

sovereign

and

secure

"infostructure".

Its

fundamental

objective

is

to

provide

the

French

armed

forces

with

a

robust,

scalable,

and

modular

technological

foundation

specifically

dedicated

to

the

ingestion

of

big

data

and

the

execution

of

complex

artificial

intelligence

(AI)

algorithms.

Technological

sovereignty

is

the

cornerstone

of

this

program.

While

civilian

massive

data

analysis

solutions

are

largely

dominated

by

non-European

actors

(mainly

American

and

Chinese),

relying

on

foreign

infrastructures

or

software

to

process

classified

data

would

expose

France

to

major

risks

of

interference,

espionage,

capture

of

strategic

information,

and

unilateral

access

disruption

in

the

event

of

a

diplomatic

crisis.

The

French

state

has

therefore

made

the

refusal

of

technological

vassalization

a

doctrine,

forbidding,

for

example,

the

deployment

of

foreign

solutions

like

those

of

the

American

firm

Palantir

within

the

ministry's

networks.

An

in-depth

analysis

of

this

sprawling

program

reveals

the

State's

will

to

structure

a

defense

industrial

and

academic

ecosystem

around

data

sovereignty

over

the

long

term.

By

studying

its

technical

architecture,

its

renewed

governance,

and

its

concrete

operational

applications

(such

as

the

Escrim

and

SIRCID

apps,

or

the

Arcadia

project),

it

is

possible

to

gain

a

nuanced

understanding

of

the

French

doctrine

regarding

military

AI.

This

expert

report

dissects

the

components

of

ARTEMIS.IA,

evaluates

its

impact

on

intelligence

services,

logistics,

and

command,

while

putting

into

perspective

the

infrastructural,

ethical,

and

human

challenges

inherent

to

such

a

technological

leap

in

the

face

of

extremely

fast

civilian

innovation

cycles.

Multidimensional

Security

and

Decompartmentalization

Management

The

DGA's

main

technical

challenge

was

to

design

an

architecture

capable

of

reconciling

two

diametrically

opposed

imperatives:

on

the

decompartmentalization

of

data

(necessary

for

AI

to

cross-reference

information

and

identify

correlations),

and

on

the

other

hand,

the

unconditional

respect

for

the

security

and

compartmentalization

of

State

secrets.

ARTEMIS.IA

solves

this

conundrum

through

a

sophisticated

"multidimensional

security"

system

natively

managing

the

"need-to-know"

principle.

Each

piece

of

data

ingested

into

the

data

lake

is

tagged

with

strict

metadata

defining

its

sensitivity

level,

source,

and

sharing

restrictions.

When

a

user

submits

a

query,

the

architecture

dynamically

filters

access

to

algorithms

and

results

based

on

the

operator

profile's

cryptographic

clearances.

This

ensures

that

an

algorithm

will

not

generate

inferences

from

data

that

the

end-user

would

not

legally

have

the

right

to

access,

ensuring

absolute

traceability

of

consultations.
